abstract: Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) during daily activities is important for various applications such as biomechanics analysis, rehabilitation, and sports science. Inertial measurement units (IMUs) have shown promise for GRF estimation due to their portability and low cost. However, the performance of IMU-based GRF estimation can be affected by the complexity of daily activities and the variability of human movements. In this study, we propose a deep learning-based GRF estimation method using a single IMU placed on the lower back. We collected IMU and GRF data from 20 healthy adults during various daily activities, including walking, running, stair climbing, and sit-to-stand transitions. We compared the performance of several deep learning models, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and hybrid CNN-RNN models. The results showed that the hybrid CNN-RNN model achieved the best performance, with a root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 0.12 BW and a correlation coefficient (R) of 0.95 for GRF estimation. We also demonstrated that the proposed method can accurately estimate GRF during different daily activities and across different subjects. These findings suggest that the proposed method has the potential to be used for real-world applications of GRF estimation.

## Summary
This paper presents a deep learning approach for estimating ground reaction forces (GRF) using a single inertial measurement unit (IMU) placed on the lower back during various daily activities. A hybrid CNN-RNN model is proposed and compared with other deep learning models. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the hybrid model in accurately estimating GRF during diverse activities and across different subjects.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   A deep learning-based GRF estimation method using a single IMU on the lower back.
*   Evaluation of CNN, RNN, and hybrid CNN-RNN models for GRF estimation.
*   Demonstration of accurate GRF estimation during various daily activities (walking, running, stair climbing, sit-to-stand).
*   Achieved state-of-the-art performance with the hybrid CNN-RNN model (RMSE of 0.12 BW and R of 0.95).
*   Showcases generalizability across different subjects.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
The work employs a deep learning architecture that effectively combines CNNs and RNNs to capture both spatial and temporal dependencies in IMU data, leading to improved GRF estimation accuracy compared to using CNNs or RNNs alone. It addresses the challenging problem of GRF estimation during diverse daily activities using only a single IMU sensor, which enhances practicality. The performance metrics reported (RMSE and R) demonstrate high accuracy, establishing the method as competitive.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   **Limited Sensor Placement:** The study uses only a single IMU placed on the lower back. Exploring other sensor locations or sensor fusion techniques with multiple IMUs might further improve accuracy.
*   **Homogeneous Population:** The study uses data from healthy adults. Testing the method on individuals with gait abnormalities or different demographics is needed to assess its robustness and generalizability.
*   **Lack of Real-time Implementation:** The paper focuses on offline GRF estimation. Evaluating and optimizing the model for real-time applications is an important next step.
*   **Limited Activity Diversity:** While several activities are included, expanding the dataset to encompass a wider range of daily living activities (e.g., bending, lifting, carrying objects) would enhance the method's practical applicability.
*   **Black Box Nature:** The deep learning models are "black boxes". Analyzing feature importance or using explainable AI techniques could provide insights into which IMU signals are most crucial for GRF estimation.
