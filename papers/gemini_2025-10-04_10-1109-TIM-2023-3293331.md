abstract: Accurate estimation of ground reaction forces (GRFs) is essential for gait analysis and biomechanical studies. Wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) offer a convenient and cost-effective solution for GRF estimation. However, the complex temporal dependencies in gait data and the variability across different individuals pose significant challenges. This paper presents a novel approach for GRF estimation based on an attention mechanism based multi-scale temporal convolutional network (AMSTCN). The AMSTCN model is designed to capture the complex temporal dependencies in gait data by employing multiple temporal convolutional layers with different kernel sizes. An attention mechanism is incorporated to adaptively weight the contributions of different temporal scales. The model is trained and evaluated using a dataset of IMU and force plate measurements collected during overground walking. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed AMSTCN model achieves state-of-the-art performance in GRF estimation compared to existing methods.

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel Attention Mechanism based Multi-Scale Temporal Convolutional Network (AMSTCN) for estimating ground reaction forces (GRFs) from IMU data. The method addresses the challenges of complex temporal dependencies and inter-subject variability in gait data by employing multi-scale temporal convolutions and attention mechanisms to adaptively weight the contributions of different temporal scales.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Proposed an AMSTCN model to capture complex temporal dependencies in gait data.
*   Utilized multiple temporal convolutional layers with different kernel sizes to process gait data at various resolutions.
*   Integrated an attention mechanism to adaptively weight the contributions of different temporal scales.
*   Achieved state-of-the-art performance in GRF estimation compared to other methods.
*   Demonstrated the effectiveness of the model on a dataset collected during overground walking.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work is state-of-the-art because it combines multi-scale temporal convolutional networks with an attention mechanism, allowing the model to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in gait data while adaptively focusing on the most relevant features. The reported results demonstrate superior performance compared to existing GRF estimation techniques, indicating an advancement in the field.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The study primarily focuses on overground walking. The model's performance on other gait activities, such as running, stair climbing, or activities of daily living is not evaluated. Future work should investigate the model's generalizability to different locomotion types.
*   The dataset used may not be diverse enough to fully represent the variability in human gait. Gathering data from a larger and more diverse population could improve the robustness and generalization of the model.
*   The paper does not explore the impact of different IMU placement locations on the model's performance. Future research could investigate optimal IMU placement strategies.
*   The computational complexity of the AMSTCN model is not explicitly addressed. Evaluating the computational cost and exploring model optimization techniques could improve the practical applicability of the approach.
