abstract: "Ground reaction force (GRF) estimation using inertial measurement units (IMUs) is gaining increasing attention for its potential in clinical and sports applications. However, the accuracy of IMU-based GRF estimation is often limited by the domain shift between different subjects, environments, and activities. Domain adaptation (DA) techniques can be used to address this issue by transferring knowledge from a source domain with labeled data to a target domain with unlabeled or limited labeled data. This paper provides a comprehensive review of DA techniques for IMU-based GRF estimation and presents a case study on the application of DA to GRF estimation across different walking speeds. The case study demonstrates that DA can significantly improve the accuracy of GRF estimation in the target domain, even with limited labeled data. ## Summary

This paper reviews domain adaptation (DA) techniques used to improve ground reaction force (GRF) estimation from inertial measurement unit (IMU) data. It highlights the problem of domain shift across subjects, environments, and activities and how DA can mitigate these issues by transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled or sparsely labeled target domain. A case study demonstrates the effectiveness of DA in improving GRF estimation accuracy across varying walking speeds.

## Key Contributions and Insights

*   Comprehensive review of domain adaptation techniques applicable to IMU-based GRF estimation.
*   Highlights the importance of addressing domain shift for accurate GRF estimation.
*   Presents a case study demonstrating the effectiveness of DA for GRF estimation across different walking speeds.
*   Demonstrates that DA can improve accuracy even with limited labeled data in the target domain.
*   Identifies challenges and potential future research directions in applying DA for GRF estimation.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art

This work is state-of-the-art because it synthesizes recent advances in domain adaptation within the context of GRF estimation, a problem directly impacted by inter-subject variability and changing environments. The explicit focus on DA techniques addresses a critical limitation of many existing GRF estimation methods, which often struggle to generalize across diverse datasets. The review aspect provides a consolidated overview, and the case study validates the practical benefits of DA. The paper considers recent machine learning approaches to domain transfer, making it relevant and current.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve

*   The case study is limited to variations in walking speed. Expanding the case study to include other factors contributing to domain shift, such as different terrains, walking styles, or subject demographics, would strengthen the findings.
*   The review could benefit from a more in-depth comparison of the reviewed DA techniques, including their computational complexity, sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, and suitability for real-time applications.
*   The paper could explore the use of unsupervised or self-supervised learning methods for domain adaptation, reducing the reliance on labeled data in the source domain.
*   Future work should investigate the integration of biomechanical models with DA techniques to further improve the accuracy and robustness of GRF estimation.
