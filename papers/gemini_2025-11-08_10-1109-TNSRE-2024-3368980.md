abstract: Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) using wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) is critical for gait analysis and biomechanics. However, generalizing GRF estimation models across diverse unseen environments remains a challenging problem. To address this challenge, we propose a novel frequency-aware deep learning framework that leverages the spectral information of IMU data to improve model generalization capability. Our framework consists of three main modules: (1) a wavelet scattering transform (WST) module that extracts discriminative and robust features from the IMU signals in the frequency domain; (2) a multi-head attention-based transformer (MHAT) module that captures long-range dependencies and temporal dynamics in the frequency-domain features; and (3) a domain adversarial training (DAT) module that minimizes the domain discrepancy between training and testing datasets. We evaluate our framework on a publicly available GRF dataset, demonstrating its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art GRF estimation methods, especially in unseen testing environments. Our results indicate that frequency-aware deep learning can significantly improve the generalizability of GRF estimation models, paving the way for more robust and reliable wearable gait analysis applications.

## Summary
This paper introduces a frequency-aware deep learning framework for estimating ground reaction forces (GRFs) from inertial measurement unit (IMU) data to improve generalization performance across different environments. The framework employs wavelet scattering transform (WST) for frequency domain feature extraction, a multi-head attention-based transformer (MHAT) to capture temporal dynamics, and domain adversarial training (DAT) to minimize domain discrepancy between training and testing data. The results show improved generalization compared to existing methods.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Proposes a novel frequency-aware deep learning framework for GRF estimation using IMU data.
*   Utilizes Wavelet Scattering Transform (WST) to extract robust frequency domain features.
*   Employs a Multi-Head Attention-based Transformer (MHAT) to capture temporal dependencies.
*   Integrates Domain Adversarial Training (DAT) to improve generalization across unseen environments.
*   Demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods on a publicly available dataset.
*   Highlights the importance of frequency information for improving the generalizability of GRF estimation models.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work is state-of-the-art because it addresses the crucial issue of generalizability in GRF estimation, which limits the real-world applicability of many existing methods. By incorporating frequency-domain analysis through wavelet scattering and domain adaptation through adversarial training, the proposed framework effectively learns domain-invariant features, making it more robust to variations in environments and walking conditions compared to previous approaches that primarily focus on time-domain features. The use of a transformer architecture with attention allows for a more effective capture of long-range temporal dependencies, leading to improved performance.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   **Computational Complexity:** Wavelet scattering and transformer models can be computationally intensive, potentially limiting real-time applicability on embedded systems. Future work should focus on optimizing the framework for reduced computational cost, possibly through model pruning or quantization.
*   **Dataset Dependency:** The performance of domain adversarial training is highly dependent on the quality and diversity of the training data. Expanding the training dataset to include a wider range of gait patterns and environmental conditions could further improve generalization.
*   **Sensor Placement Sensitivity:** The framework's performance might be sensitive to the location and orientation of the IMUs. Exploring methods for sensor calibration and robust feature extraction that are less sensitive to sensor placement could be beneficial.
*   **Lack of Real-World Validation:** The evaluation is performed on a publicly available dataset, which might not fully represent the complexities of real-world environments. Validating the framework on a larger and more diverse set of real-world data would strengthen its credibility.
*   **Limited Biomechanical Integration:** The framework relies primarily on data-driven approaches. Incorporating biomechanical knowledge or constraints could further improve the accuracy and robustness of GRF estimation.
