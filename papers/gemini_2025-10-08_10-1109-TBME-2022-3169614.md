abstract: "Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) is crucial in many biomechanical applications. Using inertial measurement units (IMUs) is a promising way to estimate GRF in real-world settings. However, GRF estimation accuracy is greatly affected by subject-specific characteristics, which limits the generalizability of existing GRF estimation models. In this paper, we propose a domain-invariant feature learning (DIFL) framework for cross-subject GRF estimation. The DIFL framework consists of a feature extractor, a GRF predictor, and a domain discriminator. The feature extractor aims to learn domain-invariant features that are robust to subject variations. The GRF predictor estimates GRF from the extracted features. The domain discriminator distinguishes the source domain (training subjects) and the target domain (testing subjects). By adversarially training the feature extractor and the domain discriminator, the feature extractor is forced to learn domain-invariant features. We evaluate the performance of the proposed DIFL framework on a publicly available dataset. The experimental results show that the DIFL framework significantly outperforms existing GRF estimation methods in terms of cross-subject generalization ability. Specifically, the DIFL framework achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.076 BW for GRF vertical component, 0.025 BW for GRF mediolateral component, and 0.023 BW for GRF anteroposterior component. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed DIFL framework for cross-subject GRF estimation."

## Summary
This paper introduces a Domain-Invariant Feature Learning (DIFL) framework to improve the cross-subject generalizability of Ground Reaction Force (GRF) estimation models using Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). The framework utilizes adversarial training to extract features robust to subject-specific variations.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   A Domain-Invariant Feature Learning (DIFL) framework is proposed for GRF estimation from IMU data.
*   The framework adversarially trains a feature extractor and a domain discriminator to learn domain-invariant features.
*   The proposed method demonstrates improved cross-subject generalization compared to existing GRF estimation methods.
*   Experimental results on a public dataset showcase the effectiveness of the DIFL framework.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work tackles the crucial problem of subject-specific variations that limit the generalizability of GRF estimation models. By employing domain-invariant feature learning through adversarial training, it aims to extract features that are less sensitive to individual differences. This approach contributes to building more robust and widely applicable GRF estimation systems, a key requirement for real-world deployment.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The method's performance might depend heavily on the diversity and quality of the training data. Future work should investigate performance with limited and biased training data.
*   The framework's complexity could be a limitation for real-time applications, especially on resource-constrained wearable devices. Exploring model compression or efficient architectures might improve this.
*   The current evaluation focuses on a specific dataset and walking conditions. Future studies should investigate the framework's performance on different datasets with varying locomotion activities.
*   Further research could explore the incorporation of subject-specific fine-tuning to personalize the domain-invariant model, combining the benefits of generalization and personalization.
