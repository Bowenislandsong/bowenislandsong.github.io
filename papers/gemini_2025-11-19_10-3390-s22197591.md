## Summary
This paper presents a deep-learning approach for multi-subject gait analysis using inertial measurement units (IMUs) to estimate ground reaction forces (GRFs). The method utilizes a CNN-LSTM architecture and sensor fusion techniques to accurately predict GRFs from IMU data collected from multiple subjects, potentially enabling low-cost and portable gait analysis systems.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   A CNN-LSTM architecture is proposed for GRF estimation using IMU data.
*   Sensor fusion techniques are employed to combine data from multiple IMUs, improving accuracy.
*   The model demonstrates promising results in multi-subject GRF estimation, indicating generalization capability.
*   The research highlights the potential for developing low-cost and portable gait analysis systems based on IMUs and deep learning.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work contributes to the state-of-the-art by effectively integrating CNNs and LSTMs for feature extraction and temporal modeling of IMU data in the context of GRF estimation. The multi-subject validation and sensor fusion further enhance the model's robustness and applicability. The focus on a potentially low-cost and portable solution aligns with current trends in wearable sensing for gait analysis.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The paper lacks detail on the specific sensor fusion technique used. Further explanation of the sensor fusion process and its contribution to performance would be beneficial.
*   The performance evaluation could be enhanced by comparing the proposed method with other existing GRF estimation techniques, especially those using similar IMU-based approaches.
*   Detailed information on the IMU sensor placement and characteristics could be included.
*   The study could benefit from exploring more advanced deep learning architectures, such as transformers, or incorporating biomechanical knowledge into the model to further improve accuracy and generalization. Investigating the impact of different gait speeds or terrains would also strengthen the findings.
