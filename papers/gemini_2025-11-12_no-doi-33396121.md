abstract: "Ground reaction forces (GRFs) are critical for gait analysis and biomechanical assessment. Wearable sensors offer a convenient alternative to traditional laboratory-based force plates. This study investigates the feasibility of real-time GRF prediction using deep learning models deployed on edge devices. We developed and evaluated several deep learning models, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), for GRF prediction using data from wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs). The models were trained on a dataset of gait data collected from healthy participants. The trained models were then deployed on edge devices, such as smartphones and embedded systems, for real-time GRF prediction. The results demonstrate that deep learning models can accurately predict GRFs in real-time using wearable IMUs and edge computing, opening up new possibilities for mobile health and personalized biomechanics. Specifically, a light CNN model was able to achieve high accuracy with minimal computational cost on an embedded device."

## Summary
This paper explores the feasibility of real-time Ground Reaction Force (GRF) prediction using deep learning models deployed on edge devices utilizing data from wearable Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). The authors train and evaluate CNN and RNN models, demonstrating the potential for accurate real-time GRF prediction for mobile health and personalized biomechanics.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Demonstrated the feasibility of real-time GRF prediction using wearable IMUs and edge computing.
*   Evaluated CNN and RNN models for GRF prediction, finding that lightweight CNN models achieve high accuracy with minimal computational cost on embedded systems.
*   Showed the potential for mobile health and personalized biomechanics applications of real-time GRF prediction.
*   The proposed lightweight CNN is suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This research advances the field by focusing on real-time GRF estimation on edge devices. Many existing studies focus on offline analysis or computationally expensive models. This paper addresses the need for efficient and accurate GRF estimation that can be deployed on wearable devices, enabling real-time feedback and applications in mobile health and sports.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The study may be limited by the size and diversity of the training dataset. Expanding the dataset to include a wider range of subjects (age, weight, gait abnormalities) and activities (running, jumping) could improve the model's generalizability.
*   The paper lacks a detailed comparison of the proposed models against other existing lightweight GRF estimation methods in terms of accuracy and computational complexity.
*   The edge device performance evaluation could be more comprehensive, including metrics like power consumption and latency under different workload conditions.

Improvements: ""
*   Investigate the use of more advanced model compression techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization) to further reduce the computational cost of the models.
*   Explore the use of sensor fusion techniques to combine IMU data with other wearable sensor data (e.g., EMG, pressure sensors) to improve GRF prediction accuracy.
*   Implement and evaluate the proposed models in a real-world application scenario, such as gait rehabilitation or sports training, to assess their practical utility.
