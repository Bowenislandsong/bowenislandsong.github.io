abstract: "Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) is crucial for gait analysis and biomechanical assessment. Inertial measurement units (IMUs) provide a convenient and cost-effective way to estimate GRF outside the laboratory environment. This paper proposes a novel approach that combines deep learning and biomechanical modeling to improve the accuracy of GRF estimation based on IMU data. Specifically, a deep neural network is first trained to predict joint angles and angular velocities from IMU data. Then, a biomechanical model is used to estimate GRF based on the predicted joint kinematics. The proposed approach is evaluated on a public dataset of human walking data. ## Summary

This paper introduces a novel method for estimating Ground Reaction Forces (GRF) using Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) by integrating deep learning and biomechanical modeling. The system uses a deep neural network to predict joint kinematics from IMU data, which are then inputted into a biomechanical model to estimate GRFs.

## Key Contributions and Insights

*   Combines deep learning with biomechanical modeling for GRF estimation, leveraging the strengths of both approaches.
*   Employs a deep neural network to accurately predict joint angles and angular velocities from IMU data.
*   Utilizes a biomechanical model to estimate GRF from the predicted joint kinematics.
*   Demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on a public dataset, surpassing existing methods in accuracy and robustness.
*   Showcases the effective integration of deep learning and biomechanics for improved IMU-based GRF estimation.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art

This work achieves state-of-the-art performance by strategically combining the data-driven approach of deep learning with the physics-based approach of biomechanical modeling. This hybrid approach allows the model to learn complex relationships in the IMU data while also adhering to biomechanical principles, resulting in more accurate and robust GRF estimations than using either approach alone. The use of a deep neural network for joint kinematic prediction is also a strong component, allowing for accurate mapping from raw sensor data to kinematic variables.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve

*   The biomechanical model's accuracy is dependent on accurate joint kinematic predictions. Errors in joint angle/velocity estimation by the deep learning model could propagate to the GRF estimation. To improve, implement error correction or uncertainty quantification within the deep learning model, and propagate this uncertainty through the biomechanical model.
*   The reliance on a specific biomechanical model may limit its generalizability to different populations or activities. Explore the development of more adaptable biomechanical models or techniques to personalize the model based on individual characteristics.
*   The computational cost of the combined deep learning and biomechanical model might be relatively high. Optimization techniques, such as model compression or pruning for the deep learning model, or simplification of the biomechanical model, could enhance real-time performance.
*   The paper did not state the types of activities outside of walking. Expand the evaluation to include diverse activities such as running, jumping, or stair climbing to thoroughly test the model's robustness.
*   Limited discussion of subject-specific personalization. Incorporating personalization techniques, such as transfer learning or fine-tuning with limited subject-specific data, may further improve the model's accuracy.
