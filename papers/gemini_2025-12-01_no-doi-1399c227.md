However, the performance of IMU-based GRF estimation models can be significantly affected by inter-subject variability and differences in gait patterns. To address this issue, we propose an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) method based on cycle-consistent learning to improve the generalization ability of GRF estimation models. The proposed method learns domain-invariant features by minimizing the distribution discrepancy between the source (labeled) and target (unlabeled) domains. ## Summary

This paper presents an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) approach for ground reaction force (GRF) estimation using inertial measurement units (IMUs). It utilizes cycle-consistent learning to mitigate the impact of inter-subject variability and gait pattern differences on model performance. The method aims to learn domain-invariant features from labeled source data and unlabeled target data, enhancing the generalization of GRF estimation models.

## Key Contributions and Insights

*   Proposes an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) method using cycle-consistent learning for GRF estimation from IMU data.
*   Learns domain-invariant features to reduce the distribution discrepancy between source and target domains.
*   Implements a cycle-consistency constraint to ensure meaningful feature learning and data structure preservation.
*   Demonstrates improved accuracy and robustness of GRF estimation compared to models without domain adaptation.
*   Validates the method on a publicly available gait dataset exhibiting domain shifts.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art

The use of cycle-consistent learning for unsupervised domain adaptation in GRF estimation addresses a critical challenge: the variability in gait patterns and inter-subject differences. By learning domain-invariant features, the method minimizes the need for labeled data from new subjects or environments, which is often a bottleneck in real-world applications. This contributes towards more robust and generalizable GRF estimation models, advancing the state-of-the-art in wearable sensor-based gait analysis.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve

*   The method's performance might be sensitive to the choice of network architecture and hyperparameters used in the cycle-consistent learning framework. To improve this, future work should focus on automated hyperparameter optimization techniques and explore different network architectures tailored for gait data.
*   The study primarily focuses on improving generalization across subjects but doesn't explicitly address variations within a single subject over time due to fatigue or changes in gait patterns. Addressing this limitation could involve incorporating adaptive learning mechanisms or online domain adaptation strategies.
*   The method could be further enhanced by integrating biomechanical knowledge or constraints into the learning process. This could improve the interpretability and accuracy of the estimated GRF. Exploring physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in conjunction with cycle-consistent learning could be a promising direction.
