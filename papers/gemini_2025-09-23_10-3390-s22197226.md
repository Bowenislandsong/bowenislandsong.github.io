This study proposes a transfer learning-based approach to estimate GRF during various locomotion activities using IMUs. A deep neural network (DNN) model was trained on a large dataset of GRF and IMU data collected from multiple subjects during walking. ## Summary

This paper presents a transfer learning approach for estimating ground reaction forces (GRF) during various locomotion activities using inertial measurement units (IMUs). A deep neural network (DNN) model is initially trained on a large dataset of walking data and then fine-tuned using smaller, subject-specific datasets encompassing activities like running, stair climbing, and jumping. The results demonstrate that this transfer learning method improves GRF estimation accuracy compared to training a DNN from scratch for each new activity or subject.

## Key Contributions and Insights

*   Demonstrates the effectiveness of transfer learning for GRF estimation across different locomotion activities.
*   Reduces the need for large, activity-specific training datasets for new subjects.
*   Improves the accuracy of GRF estimation compared to training a DNN model from scratch.
*   Utilizes a DNN architecture that can be adapted for different locomotion tasks.
*   Provides a pathway for personalized GRF estimation systems by leveraging pre-trained models.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art

This work addresses the challenge of generalizing GRF estimation models across different activities and individuals. Transfer learning enables adaptation to new activities and subjects with limited data, which is crucial for real-world applications. This approach minimizes the burden of data collection and training, a significant advantage over traditional methods requiring extensive data for each specific scenario. The use of deep learning allows for capturing complex relationships between IMU data and GRF.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve

*   The performance is likely influenced by the similarity between the source (walking) and target (other activities) data. Further investigation into optimal pre-training datasets is warranted.
*   The complexity and computational cost of the DNN might be a limitation for real-time applications on embedded systems. Explore lighter models or model compression techniques.
*   The study does not explicitly address the impact of IMU placement on transfer learning performance. Future studies could investigate optimal IMU placement strategies for different activities.
*   The dataset used for fine-tuning might not fully represent the diversity of human gait patterns. Augment the dataset with more diverse subject characteristics and gait variations.
*   The generalizability to unseen populations or pathological gaits is not fully explored. Validate the approach on a wider range of subjects, including those with gait abnormalities.
