abstract: Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) is essential for gait analysis and rehabilitation. While deep learning models have achieved promising results in GRF estimation, their generalization capability across different subjects and environments remains a challenge. This paper proposes a novel contrastive regularization framework to enhance the generalization of GRF estimation models based on inertial measurement units (IMUs). The framework introduces a contrastive loss function that encourages the model to learn domain-invariant features by minimizing the distance between representations of samples from the same GRF category but different subjects/environments, while maximizing the distance between representations of samples from different GRF categories. This regularization technique helps the model extract more robust and generalizable features from IMU data, improving its performance on unseen subjects and environments. Experimental results on a publicly available GRF dataset demonstrate that the proposed contrastive regularization framework significantly improves the generalization performance of GRF estimation models compared to existing methods.

## Summary
This paper introduces a contrastive regularization framework to improve the generalization ability of deep learning models for Ground Reaction Force (GRF) estimation from Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data. The framework uses a contrastive loss function to learn domain-invariant features, making the model more robust to variations in subjects and environments. Experimental results on a public dataset show improved generalization compared to existing methods.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Proposes a contrastive regularization framework for GRF estimation to enhance generalization.
*   Employs a contrastive loss function to minimize the distance between representations of similar GRF patterns from different subjects/environments and maximize the distance between dissimilar ones.
*   Demonstrates improved performance on unseen subjects and environments using a publicly available GRF dataset.
*   Focuses on extracting robust and generalizable features from IMU data through contrastive learning.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work addresses a crucial limitation in GRF estimation: the lack of generalization across different subjects and environments. By using contrastive learning, the model learns to focus on the essential, domain-invariant features of GRF patterns, making it more adaptable to new data. This approach represents a step forward in developing more practical and reliable GRF estimation systems for real-world applications.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The paper relies on a single publicly available dataset for evaluation. Testing on more diverse datasets, including those with different sensor placements or locomotion types, would further validate the robustness of the framework.
*   The computational cost of the contrastive learning process might be a concern for real-time applications. Investigating methods to reduce the computational complexity, such as knowledge distillation or model pruning, could be beneficial.
*   The contrastive loss function involves hyperparameters that need to be tuned. An automated or adaptive hyperparameter tuning strategy could further improve the performance and ease of use of the framework.
*   The paper focuses on generalization across subjects and environments. Investigating the performance of the framework under different sensor modalities or sensor failure scenarios could further enhance its practical utility.
