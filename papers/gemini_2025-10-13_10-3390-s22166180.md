abstract: "Ground reaction force (GRF) is a crucial parameter for gait analysis and rehabilitation. Estimating GRF using inertial measurement units (IMUs) and deep learning (DL) has emerged as a promising alternative to force plates. This paper presents a comprehensive review of the existing DL-based GRF estimation methods using IMUs, focusing on various DL architectures, sensor placements, and evaluation protocols. Furthermore, we conduct a benchmark study by implementing several representative DL models and evaluating their performance on a publicly available dataset. The benchmark results provide valuable insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different DL approaches and highlight the challenges in achieving accurate and robust GRF estimation across diverse gait conditions and populations."

## Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive review and benchmark of deep learning-based ground reaction force (GRF) estimation methods using inertial measurement units (IMUs). It analyzes different deep learning architectures, sensor placements, and evaluation protocols. It also includes a benchmark study of representative DL models on a public dataset.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   A comprehensive review of deep learning-based GRF estimation methods using IMUs.
*   Analysis of various DL architectures (CNN, RNN, Transformer, hybrid), sensor placements, and evaluation protocols.
*   A benchmark study comparing representative DL models on a publicly available dataset.
*   Identification of challenges in achieving accurate and robust GRF estimation across diverse gait conditions and populations.
*   Discussion of the strengths and weaknesses of different DL approaches.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This paper provides a current state-of-the-art overview by reviewing and benchmarking existing methodologies, which is important for establishing baseline comparisons and highlighting future research directions. The use of a public dataset for benchmarking allows for a standardized evaluation, contributing to the advancement and comparability of future research.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The benchmark is limited to the datasets used, which may not represent the full spectrum of gait patterns and population demographics. To improve, consider incorporating datasets with greater diversity in age, gait pathologies, and activity levels.
*   The review focuses primarily on deep learning methods. A comparison with traditional biomechanical modeling approaches would provide a more holistic perspective.
*   The study could benefit from investigating the impact of sensor calibration errors and noise on the accuracy of GRF estimation. Future studies should explore techniques to mitigate these effects.
*   A deeper dive into the computational cost and real-time feasibility of the different DL models would be valuable. Include measures such as inference time and memory usage for practical implementations.
