abstract: "Ground reaction force (GRF) is an essential biomechanical parameter that describes the force interaction between the human body and the supporting surface during locomotion. Wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) provide a cost-effective and convenient alternative to traditional force plates for GRF estimation. Deep learning (DL) techniques have shown promise in accurately estimating GRF from IMU data. This study comparatively evaluates the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for GRF estimation using IMU data collected during various walking and running activities. We trained and tested several CNN and RNN models with different architectures and hyperparameters on a dataset comprising IMU signals from multiple body locations and corresponding GRF measurements. Our results demonstrate that both CNN and RNN models can achieve high accuracy in GRF estimation. The CNN models exhibited superior performance in capturing local features and generating smoother GRF predictions, while the RNN models excelled in modeling temporal dependencies and handling variable-length gait cycles. We further investigated the impact of IMU placement and data preprocessing techniques on GRF estimation accuracy. Our findings suggest that optimal IMU placement and appropriate data preprocessing are crucial for maximizing the performance of DL-based GRF estimation models. This study provides valuable insights into the application of CNN and RNN models for GRF estimation using IMU data, which can facilitate the development of wearable systems for gait analysis and biomechanical assessment."

## Summary
This paper compares the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for estimating ground reaction force (GRF) using data from inertial measurement units (IMUs) during walking and running. The study evaluates different CNN and RNN architectures, examines the impact of IMU placement, and explores data preprocessing techniques.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   A comparative analysis of CNNs and RNNs for GRF estimation using IMU data.
*   Demonstration that both CNNs and RNNs can achieve high accuracy in GRF estimation.
*   CNNs excel at capturing local features and producing smoother GRF predictions.
*   RNNs excel at modeling temporal dependencies and handling variable-length gait cycles.
*   Optimal IMU placement and data preprocessing are crucial for maximizing GRF estimation accuracy.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
The paper provides a comparative analysis of two commonly used deep learning architectures in the context of GRF estimation. It systematically evaluates CNNs and RNNs, which allows for a better understanding of their strengths and weaknesses for this specific task. The research is recent and aligns with the trend of using deep learning for biomechanical parameter estimation.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The paper primarily focuses on walking and running activities. Expanding the analysis to other locomotion activities, such as stair climbing or jumping, could broaden the applicability of the findings.
*   The paper investigates different model architectures, but the hyperparameter tuning might not be exhaustive. Utilizing more advanced optimization techniques, such as Bayesian optimization or grid search, could potentially lead to improved performance.
*   The study could benefit from an investigation into the model's generalizability to unseen subjects and environments. Implementing domain adaptation techniques or transfer learning could improve the robustness of the models.
*   A more detailed analysis of the computational cost and real-time feasibility of the different models would be beneficial for practical applications.
