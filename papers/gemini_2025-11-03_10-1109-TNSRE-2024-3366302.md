abstract: "Accurate and personalized estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) is essential for various applications in rehabilitation and sports science. However, collecting sufficient subject-specific data for training individual models is often challenging. Federated learning (FL) offers a promising solution by enabling collaborative model training without sharing raw data. This paper proposes an adaptive federated learning framework for personalized GRF estimation using inertial measurement units (IMUs). The framework incorporates an adaptive optimization strategy to address the heterogeneity in data distributions across different subjects and walking conditions. Specifically, we introduce a personalized weighting mechanism that adjusts the contribution of each client’s local updates based on the similarity between the client’s data distribution and the global model's knowledge. Furthermore, we employ a meta-learning approach to learn optimal initialization parameters for each client, facilitating faster convergence and improved personalization. Experimental results on a publicly available GRF dataset demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves superior performance compared to traditional FL methods and centralized learning approaches, particularly in scenarios with limited subject-specific data. ## Summary
This paper introduces an adaptive federated learning (FL) framework designed to improve personalized ground reaction force (GRF) estimation using data from inertial measurement units (IMUs). The framework addresses the challenge of data heterogeneity across different subjects and walking conditions through an adaptive optimization strategy and meta-learning initialization. Experimental results demonstrate improved GRF estimation accuracy compared to traditional FL and centralized learning methods, especially when subject-specific data is limited.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   An adaptive federated learning framework for personalized GRF estimation using IMU data.
*   A personalized weighting mechanism that adjusts the contribution of each client’s local updates based on data distribution similarity.
*   A meta-learning approach to learn optimal initialization parameters for each client, enabling faster convergence and better personalization.
*   Demonstrated improved GRF estimation accuracy compared to traditional FL and centralized learning, particularly with limited subject-specific data.
*   Addresses the challenge of data heterogeneity in FL for GRF estimation.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
*   It leverages federated learning to address the practical challenges of collecting sufficient subject-specific data for GRF estimation, respecting data privacy.
*   The adaptive weighting mechanism and meta-learning initialization are novel approaches to handle data heterogeneity in federated learning, which is a significant challenge in real-world applications.
*   It builds upon recent advancements in FL and deep learning techniques for biomechanical signal processing.
*   The experimental evaluation includes a comparison against standard methods, demonstrating the superiority of the proposed framework.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The framework's performance may be sensitive to the choice of similarity metric used in the personalized weighting mechanism. Investigating alternative similarity metrics or adaptive methods for selecting the best metric could improve robustness.
*   The meta-learning approach for initialization parameter learning could be computationally expensive. Exploring more efficient meta-learning algorithms or techniques for reducing the computational burden would be beneficial.
*   The evaluation is limited to a single publicly available dataset. Evaluating the framework on diverse datasets with varying IMU placements, subject populations, and walking conditions would provide a more comprehensive assessment of its generalizability.
*   The study does not explicitly address the issue of concept drift, where the relationship between IMU data and GRF changes over time. Incorporating mechanisms to detect and adapt to concept drift could further enhance the long-term performance of the framework.
*   The framework relies on labeled data for initial training. Exploring semi-supervised or unsupervised federated learning approaches could reduce the reliance on labeled data and improve its applicability in scenarios with limited labeled data.
