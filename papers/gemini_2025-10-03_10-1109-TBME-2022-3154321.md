## Summary
This paper proposes a federated learning (FL) approach for estimating ground reaction forces (GRF) from inertial measurement units (IMUs). It addresses data privacy concerns and data heterogeneity by training a global GRF estimation model collaboratively across multiple participants without sharing raw IMU data. The performance of the federated model is comparable to or better than a centrally trained model.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Developed a federated learning framework for GRF estimation using IMU data.
*   Demonstrated the feasibility of collaborative GRF estimation without sharing raw data.
*   Addressed data heterogeneity across different users via federated averaging.
*   Showed comparable or improved performance compared to centralized training.
*   Provided privacy-preserving GRF estimation.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
*   Utilizes federated learning, a state-of-the-art technique for privacy-preserving machine learning.
*   Addresses the challenge of data heterogeneity in GRF estimation across different individuals.
*   Offers a decentralized approach that avoids the need for a central data repository.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   Communication costs associated with federated learning can be high, especially with many participants. Exploring techniques like model compression or selective client participation could improve efficiency.
*   The current framework might be vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Implementing robust aggregation methods or differential privacy techniques can enhance security.
*   The impact of non-IID data distributions across clients could be further investigated. Stratified federated learning or personalized federated learning techniques can mitigate these issues.
