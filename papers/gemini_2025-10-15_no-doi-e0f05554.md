abstract: "Accurate estimation of ground reaction force (GRF) using wearable sensors has great potential for a variety of applications. However, a deep learning (DL) model trained on one dataset tends to perform poorly on data collected from new subjects or environments, which limits its practical use. To address this problem, we propose an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) method based on a variational autoencoder (VAE) to improve the generalization performance of GRF estimation. The proposed method attempts to extract domain-invariant features from the IMU data by using a VAE with adversarial learning. Specifically, we train the VAE to reconstruct the input IMU data and to generate domain-confused representations. Furthermore, a regressor is trained on the latent space of the VAE to predict GRF. The whole framework is trained end-to-end. The proposed method is validated on a publicly available dataset. The results show that the proposed method can effectively reduce the domain gap between different subjects and improve the generalization performance of GRF estimation. ## Summary
This paper presents an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) method using a variational autoencoder (VAE) to improve the generalization of Ground Reaction Force (GRF) estimation from Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data across different subjects. The VAE extracts domain-invariant features, which are then used to predict GRF. The model is trained end-to-end and validated on a public dataset, demonstrating improved accuracy and robustness compared to other domain adaptation methods.

## Key Contributions and Insights
*   Proposes an unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) framework for GRF estimation using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
*   Employs adversarial learning within the VAE to extract domain-invariant features from IMU data, reducing the subject-specific bias.
*   Demonstrates improved generalization performance compared to other domain adaptation techniques for GRF estimation.
*   The framework is trained end-to-end, simplifying the optimization process.

## Why this is State-of-the-Art
This work addresses a critical challenge in GRF estimation - the lack of generalization across different subjects. Using unsupervised domain adaptation with a VAE is a sophisticated approach to extract features that are less sensitive to individual variations, pushing the boundaries of GRF estimation models towards practical applications. The end-to-end training methodology further improves the simplicity and efficiency.

## Weaknesses or Limitations and How to Improve
*   The study focuses primarily on inter-subject variability. The method's effectiveness might be limited when dealing with changes in activity types or sensor placement. Future work could explore domain adaptation across different activities or sensor locations.
*   The reliance on a single publicly available dataset limits the generalizability of the conclusions. Validation on multiple datasets and real-world scenarios would strengthen the findings.
*   The paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of the latent space learned by the VAE. Understanding what specific features are being captured and how they contribute to domain invariance would be valuable. Furthermore, visualizing this latent space could be included for increased insight into the performance of the VAE.
*   While the paper focuses on unsupervised DA, exploring semi-supervised DA with limited labeled target data might lead to better performance.
