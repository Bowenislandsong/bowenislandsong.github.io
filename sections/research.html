Dissertation

Scalable Biomechanical Modeling Through Federated Learning and Resource Federation

Ph.D. in Computer Science, University of Southern California, June 2026. Advisor: Prof. Leana Golubchik. Defense: June 18, 2026. Committee members listed on the defense materials: Prof. Mahdi Soltanolkotabi (CS) and Prof. Jill L. McNitt-Gray (Biomechanics).

Dissertation PDF · Defense slides

Part I · Modeling — SVD Embedding Regression (SER)

Estimates ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables from IMU signals by learning a linear map between low-rank SVD embeddings of inputs and outputs. On running data, SER and KNN match or exceed LSTM accuracy at a fraction of the cost—about 0.3 ms for 10 steps on a smartphone versus ~15 s for an LSTM in the dissertation evaluation.

Part II · Data — Federated SER (FedSER)

Federated multi-task extension for GRFs and hip/knee joint moments across participants and actions. Learns shared SVD bases without centralizing raw data; combines action-specific experts with a global expert. Matches centralized SER, outperforms TCN baselines under scarcity and leave-one-action-out, and was timed on a Pixel 8 Pro in an 11-client setup.

Part III · Resources — Hybrid cloud federations

Models a federation of hybrid clouds as a continuous-time Markov chain under QoS requirements. Proves full sharing is optimal under homogeneous QoS, shows heterogeneous QoS changes the picture, and allocates public-cloud savings via Shapley value to discourage free riding. A systems study separate from the biomechanics experiments.

Research themes

Constraints that shaped the dissertation.

Edge-deployable embodied ML

Interpretable, physically meaningful estimators that stay efficient enough for phones and other edge devices. Wearable and motion data are a demanding testbed: models must respect biomechanical structure, stay legible, and still run when the person, sensor placement, and environment change.

Personalization with privacy built in

Adapt across users, devices, and actions without brittle retraining or centralizing sensitive signals. Lightweight adaptation, multi-task experts, and federated learning matter because average-case performance is often the wrong target, and private wearable or clinical data rarely lives in one clean training loop.

Resource federation under QoS

When organizations keep data local but need bursty compute, when does sharing machines help—and how are savings allocated fairly? Clinics and labs are a natural audience for this systems question. The cloud chapter is intentionally separate from the FedSER experiments.

  1. B. Song, M. Paolieri, H. E. Stewart, L. Golubchik, J. L. McNitt-Gray, V. Misra, and D. Shah, “Estimating Ground Reaction Forces From Inertial Sensors,” IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, vol. 72, no. 2, pp. 595–608, 2025.

    [PDF] [DOI]

    We study whether inertial measurement units collected during steady-state running can estimate ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables with lightweight models. The paper proposes SVD Embedding Regression (SER) and shows that low-rank methods can match or beat LSTM baselines while remaining practical for edge deployment and personalization. SER compresses IMU and GRF waveforms into low-rank embeddings, learns a linear map in that space, then reconstructs force signals.

    [PDF] [DOI]
