Research

Research on machine learning systems that get more compelling when the constraints get harder.

My research is not a loose collection of topics. It is a consistent attempt to make intelligent systems more deployable, more adaptive, and more trustworthy by taking real constraints seriously instead of treating them as edge cases.

I work in the QED Research Group at USC Viterbi with Leana Golubchik. My PhD uses embodied and wearable ML as a demanding testbed for broader questions about personalization, efficiency, privacy, and evaluation. The same instincts also show up in my systems work on cloud federation, synchronization, and production-minded infrastructure.

The publication record reflects a pattern I care about. In my GRF paper, I challenged the assumption that heavier sequence models were necessary and showed that a low-rank, interpretable method could match or exceed them while being deployable on edge hardware. In my CLOUD paper, I modeled QoS-constrained hybrid cloud federation with explicit incentives. In my Allerton paper, I took an algorithmic idea all the way to real-workload evidence against rsync.

That is why I believe this work transfers beyond biomechanics. Teams building modern AI systems still need the same core strengths: strong experimental taste, careful evaluation, implementation discipline, and the judgment to know when the simpler or more principled solution is actually the better one.

Research Agenda

The research questions underneath the publications.

Embodied intelligence that can leave the lab

I am interested in models that turn wearable and motion signals into clinically or operationally meaningful estimates without requiring oversized networks or lab-only sensing setups. That means choosing representations and architectures that do not collapse the moment the subject, sensor placement, or environment changes.

The impact here is practical: if the model is fast, interpretable, and stable enough, it can move from a paper result to something athletes, clinicians, or assistive systems can actually use. More broadly, it is a useful proving ground for any AI system that has to infer structure from messy human-centered signals and then earn trust from a real user.

Personalization without brittle retraining

The strongest systems I have seen adapt to the person, the device, and the environment. That is why I keep returning to lightweight adaptation, few-shot personalization, and federated learning. I want systems that improve their fit to the user without paying an unrealistic price in data collection, retraining cost, or privacy risk.

This matters because average-case performance is often the wrong target when the real problem is heterogeneity across users, settings, and hardware. I think this question will keep mattering as AI products become more interactive, more persistent, and more personal.

Research systems that survive contact with reality

I care about infrastructure, evaluation, and operational discipline because they shape what research can become in practice. This is the through-line from cloud theory to ranking to OpenShift. I am interested in systems that make research faster to run, easier to trust, and harder to fool with accidental shortcuts.

The impact is leverage: a good system makes future research faster, more reproducible, and much easier for a team to extend. In many environments, that leverage matters as much as any single model improvement.

IEEE TBME Health AI 2024

Estimating Ground Reaction Forces From Inertial Sensors

B. Song, M. Paolieri, H. E. Stewart, L. Golubchik, J. L. McNitt-Gray, V. Misra, D. Shah

Introduces SVD Embedding Regression, a low-rank and interpretable alternative to heavy sequence models for latency-sensitive wearable sensing.

The result matters beyond biomechanics because it shows a research instinct I trust: when the deployment setting is constrained, a smaller and clearer model can be more valuable than a heavier one with marginal gains. I care about results like this because they create actual options for deployment instead of just nicer figures in a benchmark table.

Signal: I challenge the assumption that a bigger sequence model is automatically the stronger answer.

PDF IEEE Xplore

We study whether inertial measurement units collected during steady-state running can estimate ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables with lightweight models. The paper proposes SVD Embedding Regression and shows that low-rank methods can match or beat LSTM baselines while remaining practical for edge deployment and personalization.

IEEE CLOUD Cloud systems 2022

Performance and Revenue Analysis of Hybrid Cloud Federations with QoS Requirements

Bowen Song, Marco Paolieri, Leana Golubchik

A queueing-theoretic and incentive-aware framework for understanding when federated hybrid clouds improve QoS, cost, and fairness.

This work matters because large systems only improve when the incentives and resource tradeoffs are actually modeled well; otherwise the engineering looks elegant but the deployment collapses under competing objectives. It shows that I am comfortable reasoning about multi-agent systems, mechanism design, and operational tradeoffs, not only training a model and reporting metrics.

Signal: I can combine formal modeling, incentives, and systems thinking in one research contribution.

PDF IEEE Xplore

This paper analyzes hybrid cloud federations where members share local resources and distribute savings according to Shapley value. It characterizes when full sharing is optimal, when heterogeneous QoS makes the problem harder, and how to discourage free riders without central trust.

Allerton Distributed data 2019

Scalable String Reconciliation by Recursive Content-Dependent Shingling (RCDS)

Bowen Song, Ari Trachtenberg

A communication-efficient reconciliation protocol for large, incrementally changing data, evaluated against real revision histories from popular repositories.

The broader value is that it turns a theoretical problem into a systems result that can be benchmarked against widely used tools, which is the kind of translation from concept to evidence that I find most satisfying. I still value this paper because it captures a habit that has stayed constant in my work: prove enough to trust the idea, then implement enough to learn where reality pushes back.

Signal: I can take an algorithmic idea all the way to real-workload evidence against a standard tool.

PDF IEEE Xplore

RCDS addresses the remote reconciliation of similar strings with low communication cost. It scales linearly with edit distance, is practical on large strings, and outperforms rsync on a substantial fraction of realistic workloads.
