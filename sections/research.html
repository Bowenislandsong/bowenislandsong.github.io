Dissertation PDF Publications

Part I · Modeling

SVD Embedding Regression (SER)

Estimates ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables from IMU signals by learning a linear map between low-rank SVD embeddings of inputs and outputs. On running data, SER and KNN match or exceed LSTM accuracy at a fraction of the cost — about 0.3 ms for 10 steps on a smartphone versus ~15 s for an LSTM in the dissertation evaluation.

Part II · Data

Federated SER (FedSER)

Federated multi-task extension for GRFs and hip/knee joint moments across participants and actions. Learns shared SVD bases without centralizing raw data; combines action-specific experts with a global expert. Matches centralized SER, outperforms TCN baselines under scarcity and leave-one-action-out, and was timed on a Pixel 8 Pro in an 11-client setup.

Part III · Resources

Hybrid cloud federations

Models a federation of hybrid clouds as a continuous-time Markov chain under QoS requirements. Proves full sharing is optimal under homogeneous QoS, shows heterogeneous QoS changes the picture, and allocates public-cloud savings via Shapley value to discourage free riding. A systems study separate from the biomechanics experiments.

Research Themes

Constraints that shaped the dissertation.

Edge-deployable embodied ML

Interpretable, physically meaningful estimators that stay efficient enough for phones and other edge devices.

Wearable and motion data are a demanding testbed: models must respect biomechanical structure, stay legible, and still run when the person, sensor placement, and environment change.

Personalization with privacy built in

Adapt across users, devices, and actions without brittle retraining or centralizing sensitive signals.

Lightweight adaptation, multi-task experts, and federated learning matter because average-case performance is often the wrong target, and private wearable or clinical data rarely lives in one clean training loop.

Resource federation under QoS

When organizations keep data local but need bursty compute, when does sharing machines help — and how are savings allocated fairly?

Clinics and labs are a natural audience for this systems question. The cloud chapter is intentionally separate from the FedSER experiments.

IEEE TBME Health AI 2025

Estimating Ground Reaction Forces From Inertial Sensors

B. Song, M. Paolieri, H. E. Stewart, L. Golubchik, J. L. McNitt-Gray, V. Misra, D. Shah

Introduces SVD Embedding Regression (SER): compress IMU and GRF waveforms into low-rank embeddings, learn a linear map in that space, then reconstruct force signals. On collegiate distance-running data with shank and sacrum IMUs, SER and KNN match or exceed LSTM accuracy while remaining far cheaper to train and run — supporting edge deployment and personalization with limited personal data.

Takeaway: a bigger sequence model is not automatically better when latency, memory, and interpretability matter.

PDF IEEE Xplore

Evaluated across input combinations (acceleration and angular velocity at multiple locations) and data scenarios (personal / others / everyone). Joint multimodal sensing and personal training data both reduce error; discrete variables such as contact time and impulse benefit especially from personalization. IEEE Trans. Biomed. Eng., 72(2), 2025 (DOI year 2024).

We study whether inertial measurement units collected during steady-state running can estimate ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables with lightweight models. The paper proposes SVD Embedding Regression and shows that low-rank methods can match or beat LSTM baselines while remaining practical for edge deployment and personalization.

Dissertation chapter Federated learning 2026

FedSER for Wearable Biomechanics

Dissertation Chapter 4 · Federated multi-task biomechanical estimation

Extends SER to a federated multi-task setting for estimating GRFs and hip/knee joint moments across participants and actions. Clients collaboratively learn shared SVD bases and embedding regressors without sharing raw waveforms; a federated activity classifier gates action-specific experts with a global expert. On a public lower-limb biomechanics dataset, FedSER matches centralized SER and outperforms TCN baselines under data scarcity and leave-one-action-out. In an 11-client experiment (one physical Pixel 8 Pro + ten simulated clients), phone training finished in ~3 minutes versus ~40.8 minutes for a centralized TCN on an A100; inference was 0.16 ms/sample vs 71 ms for the TCN.

Takeaway: federated low-rank models can keep accuracy while respecting privacy and edge compute limits.

Dissertation overview

Compatible with secure aggregation; retains accuracy comparable to non-private TCN under differential privacy dataset release in the reported experiments. Also studies projection-based recovery when individual IMU channels are missing at test time. Presented as Part II of the June 2026 defense; not listed here as a separate peer-reviewed venue unless/until published.

FedSER learns shared low-rank structure and multi-task experts across clients for wearable biomechanical estimation without centralizing raw data, targeting accuracy, privacy, and on-device practicality together.

IEEE CLOUD Cloud systems 2022

Performance and Revenue Analysis of Hybrid Cloud Federations with QoS Requirements

Bowen Song, Marco Paolieri, Leana Golubchik

Analyzes hybrid cloud federations where members share local capacity and overflow to public clouds under quality-of-service constraints. Uses a continuous-time Markov chain performance model, characterizes when full sharing is optimal (homogeneous QoS) versus when heterogeneous QoS changes the answer, and divides public-cloud savings with Shapley value so both workload and resource contributions are rewarded.

Takeaway: formal modeling, incentives, and systems thinking in one federation contribution.

PDF IEEE Xplore

Forms Chapter 5 of the dissertation. Queueing analysis, QoS, and revenue sharing are treated as one federation problem rather than separate side issues.

This paper analyzes hybrid cloud federations where members share local resources and distribute savings according to Shapley value. It characterizes when full sharing is optimal, when heterogeneous QoS makes the problem harder, and how to discourage free riders without central trust.

Allerton Distributed data 2019

Scalable String Reconciliation by Recursive Content-Dependent Shingling (RCDS)

Bowen Song, Ari Trachtenberg

A communication-efficient protocol for reconciling similar large strings by transferring differences rather than full payloads. Scales with edit distance, remains practical on large strings, and was validated on real revision histories — outperforming rsync on a substantial fraction of realistic workloads.

Takeaway: take an algorithmic idea all the way to real-workload evidence against a standard tool.

PDF IEEE Xplore

Earlier work with Ari Trachtenberg; Go implementation available as RCDS_GO for large-file synchronization experiments.

RCDS addresses the remote reconciliation of similar strings with low communication cost. It scales linearly with edit distance, is practical on large strings, and outperforms rsync on a substantial fraction of realistic workloads.
