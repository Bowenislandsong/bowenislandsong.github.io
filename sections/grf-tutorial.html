Fundamentals

Biomechanics concepts you need first

Kinetics vs kinematics

Kinematics describes motion (joint angles, angular velocity, acceleration). Kinetics explains the causes of motion (forces and moments). GRF and joint moments are kinetic variables.

Sagittal-plane assumptions

This tutorial uses a 2D sagittal model for clarity. Hip and knee are treated as hinge-like in flexion/extension, while frontal/transverse-plane terms are ignored in the first pass.

Free-body diagrams

For each segment, draw all external forces, the segment weight, and unknown joint reaction/moment terms. Inverse dynamics solves these unknowns from distal to proximal.

Anthropometrics

Segment mass fraction, COM location, and segment moment of inertia strongly affect results. Use one published anthropometric table consistently across subjects.

Core Equations

Linear dynamics: \(\sum \mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}_{COM}\)

Angular dynamics: \(\sum M_j = I_j\alpha + \mathbf{r}_{COM/j} \times m\mathbf{a}_{COM}\)

Vertical GRF approximation: \(F_z \approx m(a_z + g)\)

Knee moment (2D distal-to-proximal): \(M_{knee} = I_{shank}\alpha_{shank} + \mathbf{r}_{COM/knee} \times m_{shank}\mathbf{a}_{COM} - \mathbf{r}_{GRF/knee} \times \mathbf{F}_{GRF}\)

For the hip, apply the same balance on the thigh using known knee reaction terms from the previous step. Keep flexion-extension sign definitions fixed throughout the entire pipeline.
