  1. B. Song, M. Paolieri, H. E. Stewart, L. Golubchik, J. L. McNitt-Gray, V. Misra, and D. Shah, “Estimating Ground Reaction Forces From Inertial Sensors,” IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, vol. 72, no. 2, pp. 595–608, 2025.

    [PDF] [DOI]

    We study whether inertial measurement units collected during steady-state running can estimate ground reaction forces and derived biomechanical variables with lightweight models. The paper proposes SVD Embedding Regression (SER) and shows that low-rank methods can match or beat LSTM baselines while remaining practical for edge deployment and personalization. SER compresses IMU and GRF waveforms into low-rank embeddings, learns a linear map in that space, then reconstructs force signals.

  2. B. Song, “FedSER for Wearable Biomechanics,” Chapter 4 in Scalable Biomechanical Modeling Through Federated Learning and Resource Federation, Ph.D. dissertation, University of Southern California, 2026.

    [PDF] [Dissertation]

    Federated multi-task extension of SER for estimating GRFs and hip/knee joint moments across participants and actions without centralizing raw data. Matches centralized SER, outperforms TCN baselines under data scarcity and leave-one-action-out, and was timed on a Pixel 8 Pro in an 11-client setup (~3 min training on phone vs ~40.8 min for a centralized TCN on an A100). Not listed as a separate peer-reviewed venue unless/until published.

  3. B. Song, M. Paolieri, and L. Golubchik, “Performance and Revenue Analysis of Hybrid Cloud Federations with QoS Requirements,” in Proc. IEEE International Conference on Cloud Computing (CLOUD), 2022.

    [PDF] [DOI]

    Analyzes hybrid cloud federations where members share local resources and distribute savings according to Shapley value. Characterizes when full sharing is optimal under homogeneous QoS, when heterogeneous QoS changes the answer, and how to discourage free riders without central trust. Forms Chapter 5 of the dissertation.

  4. B. Song and A. Trachtenberg, “Scalable String Reconciliation by Recursive Content-Dependent Shingling,” in Proc. 57th Annual Allerton Conference on Communication, Control, and Computing (Allerton), 2019.

    [PDF] [IEEE]

    A communication-efficient protocol for reconciling similar large strings by transferring differences rather than full payloads. Scales with edit distance, remains practical on large strings, and was validated on real revision histories—outperforming rsync on a substantial fraction of realistic workloads.
