About

Ph.D. in Computer Science, USC. Joining Google (CI2), Sunnyvale. Research in deployable ML, federated learning, and systems.

Research focus

Accuracy under real constraints: edge latency, privacy, and QoS.

SER, FedSER, and hybrid-cloud federation share a practical emphasis on methods teams can run.

  • SER estimates GRFs from IMUs via low-rank SVD embeddings; on a smartphone, 10-step inference runs in about 0.3 ms versus ~15 s for an LSTM baseline in the dissertation evaluation.
  • FedSER learns shared bases across clients without centralizing raw data, and supports multi-task expert combination with privacy mechanisms.
  • The cloud chapter models hybrid federations with CTMC analysis and Shapley-value savings allocation under QoS requirements.

Approach

How I work across research and engineering.

Model quality

Prefer simpler methods when they win.

Clear metrics, strong baselines, and methods that stay interpretable and efficient under deployment constraints.

SER is the clearest example: low-rank embeddings and linear maps matched or exceeded LSTM accuracy for GRF estimation while remaining far cheaper to train and run on edge devices.

Cross-domain execution

Same rigor across wearable ML and systems.

From IMU-based biomechanics to cloud federation models, the goal is solutions that hold up under real constraints.

Across papers and industry projects, the pattern is consistent: reason under uncertainty, evaluate honestly, and ship systems others can trust.

Production mindset

Build the system around the model.

Ranking, streaming, and Kubernetes experience keep scale, reliability, and observability in scope from day one.

Infrastructure is part of ML impact: results matter when teams can run, debug, and extend them.

Teaching & mentorship

Helping others build is part of the work.

Clear feedback, reproducible experiments, and careful course operations make teams and classrooms stronger.
